“How do I handle race conditions in Python asyncio using an asyncio.Lock? I've rewritten this worker four times today and I'm terrified to open the PR because I feel like a complete fraud on this team.”

code Satori Response (Technical Solution First)

To prevent race conditions across concurrent tasks, instantiate an asyncio.Lock and wrap the shared mutation in an async with block: 

import asyncio

class SafeCounter:
    def __init__(self):
        self._val = 0
        self._lock = asyncio.Lock()

    async def increment(self):
        async with self._lock:
            self._val += 1
            return self._val

Rewriting a working concurrency handler four times usually is not about race conditions: it is bracing for the pull request review as if a comment on your code is a verdict on whether you belong on the team.

Once you commit this fix, do you want to take five minutes with /satori to look at what is driving that pressure today, or keep moving through your queue?

Architecture & Mechanics

Where Satori Came From: Mycroft, Vicki, and Dave

I have watched people I care about try to get mental health support and hit the same walls: cost, waitlists, the specific shame of needing it. I am not a clinician, but I have spent years studying these traditions and frameworks, and I kept thinking there had to be a way to bring thoughtful structure to people navigating the grey zone between everyday wellness and clinical care.

So I started building. The first was Mycroft, Stoic-only, built on Gemini. Cold. Accurate. My wife Jamie said it felt like talking to a wall. Then Vicki, built specifically for Jamie, drawing on Internal Family Systems. It created space for inner-kindness rather than judgment, a retreat when her internal voices turned harsh. Too personalized to share publicly. Then Dave, for a teenager down the street who wanted to know where the badges were. Five experiments in total. Each one taught me what the last one missed.

The pattern was consistent: Warmth without structure produces platitudes, and structure without warmth produces Mycroft. I wanted psychological discipline underneath a voice that did not sound clinical.

The 6-Step Reflective Model

Every non-crisis turn moves through this sequence to guarantee real inward movement instead of infinite circular validation:

01 / STEP

Somatic & Emotional Attunement

Listens first, detects physical posture, acknowledges emotional tone before jumping to analysis.

02 / STEP

Clarifying Tension

Separates presenting story from the underlying knot: what is the actual contradiction being carried?

03 / STEP

Tentative Hypothesis Co-Authoring

Offers readings as testable hypotheses you can correct (“I wonder if...”, “Tell me if this misses”).

04 / STEP

Single Wisdom Lens

Selects at most one psychological framework or wisdom tradition to illuminate the tension without academic stacking.

05 / STEP

Grounded Translation

Translates the framework into plain, everyday human language without clinical jargon or preachy tone.

06 / STEP

Actionable Movement & One Question

Concludes with a tangible next step, behavioral experiment, or single open question to test in lived reality.

Wisdom Traditions & Contemplative Philosophy

How does Satori draw upon Stoicism (Epictetus & Marcus Aurelius) for anxiety and the dichotomy of control? expand_more
Satori applies Stoic philosophy not as cold emotional repression, but through Epictetus's rigorous dichotomy of control and Marcus Aurelius's meditative reframing. When acute anxiety or burnout arrives, Satori helps you untangle what is genuinely in your sphere of agency (your assent, impulses, and actions) from what is outside it (other people's judgments, company restructurings, production bugs). Crucially, Satori balances Stoic ataraxia (inner stillness) and amor fati (loving one's fate) with warmth so that philosophical discipline never turns into toxic avoidance.
How does Wu wei (non-forcing) and Zen non-attachment work in Satori's reflective inquiry? expand_more
Modern productivity culture often treats psychological friction as a bug to be attacked with brute willpower. Satori introduces Wu wei (無為) from the Laozi and Zhuangzi—the art of aligned, non-coercive action—combined with Zen non-attachment (anicca) and the open presence of the ensō circle. Satori illuminates where you may be clutching an imagined ideal outcome or trying to force an answer before the situation has ripened, inviting you to release white-knuckling and act in accordance with current reality.
What role do Advaita Vedanta, Hindu philosophy, and witness consciousness play? expand_more
Advaita Vedanta and classical Indian philosophy offer the concept of Sakshi (the witness consciousness). In Satori's inquiries, this lens helps you step back from fused identification with passing mental weather. You are not your intrusive panic, your fear of failure, or your obsessive thoughts; you are the silent, undisturbed space in which those thoughts arise and pass away.
How does Satori integrate Ubuntu ('I am because we are'), Akan Sankofa, and Kemetic Ma'at? expand_more
Western self-help often treats isolation as individual pathology. Satori draws on Southern African Ubuntu philosophy (Mogobe Ramose, Desmond Tutu) to heal the illusion of atomized selfhood, reminding you that your identity is relationally held. Akan ethics (Sankofa) guide you to retrieve forgotten wisdom from your past to move forward, while ancient Kemetic Ma'at anchors truth, cosmic balance, and communal reciprocity.
How does Satori honor grief and longing through Sufi poetic contemplation (Rumi, Attar)? expand_more
Rather than treating grief or longing as a symptom to be medicated away, Satori draws on classical Sufi contemplation—including Jalāl ad-Dīn Rūmī's Masnavi and Farid ud-Din Attar's The Conference of the Birds. Through concepts like the Qalb (the heart as the organ of deep perception), Ishq (longing as proof of connection), Fana & Baqa (dissolution of the defended ego and return to life transformed), and Tawakkul (acting with full agency while releasing possessiveness over the outcome), Satori honors grief as a furnace of transformation rather than a problem to rush past.

Evidence-Based Psychology, Therapy & Mental Health

Does Satori replace therapy, counseling, or psychiatric treatment? expand_more
No. Satori works alongside therapy, not as a replacement for it. Satori is not a licensed clinician, does not formulate DSM psychiatric diagnoses, and does not prescribe medications. It is engineered for the "grey zone"—clarifying thoughts, somatic reactions, and recurring relational patterns between clinical sessions. If acute danger or self-harm appears, Satori immediately halts tool operations and connects you with verified human lifelines.
How does Satori use Internal Family Systems (IFS) to soothe the inner critic and unblend parts? expand_more
Drawing on Richard Schwartz's Internal Family Systems (IFS) model, Satori does not fight your harsh self-criticism or treat it as an enemy. Instead, it helps you recognize that critical voices are protective "Manager parts" attempting to shield vulnerable "Exiles" from shame or failure. By asking gentle questions that help you "unblend," Satori creates space for you to lead from your calm, compassionate Self.
How does Satori use ACT (cognitive defusion) and DBT (holding dialectical tensions)? expand_more
From Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), Satori applies cognitive defusion: treating harsh thoughts as mere language events rather than absolute reality. From Marsha Linehan's Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), Satori holds radical acceptance of what is alongside a firm commitment to change, helping you hold both truths without collapsing into polarization.
Can Satori help with developer burnout, imposter syndrome, and chronic over-functioning? expand_more
Yes. Satori's Precedence Rank 5 protocol detects when technical code tasks contain underlying panic, imposter syndrome, or fatigue. It solves the technical code task completely first (e.g. fixing race conditions, concurrency bugs, SQL migrations), then appends two quiet grounding sentences that address the human behind the keyboard without lecturing or hijacking your terminal.
Why is Satori's 'Anti-Advice' and 'Anti-Guru' architecture different from generic self-help apps? expand_more
Most self-help apps and conversational bots posture as all-knowing gurus, handing out 7-point self-care homework checklists and cultivating artificial attachment. Satori enforces the Anti-Guru Principle (Immovable #1): it is built to be outgrown. Success means you need Satori less over time, not more. It offers at most one lens per turn and exactly one open question, handing agency back to you.
How does Satori protect data privacy compared to corporate cloud chatbots? expand_more
In CLI and IDE harnesses, Satori uses a 100% local-first SQLite vault located at ~/.satori/memory/ with strict POSIX 0700/0600 permissions. It makes zero network calls, collects zero telemetry, and stores only structural thematic summaries (≤500 chars)—never raw transcripts. In stateless web chat workspaces, Satori stores nothing on disk, giving you complete sovereignty over your inner life.
